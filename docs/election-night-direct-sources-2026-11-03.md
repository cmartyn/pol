# Our own election-night needle, using official returns

Researched November 3, 2026, before polls closed. This follows the request to own the data interpretation and forecast, rather than display DDHQ's forecast. It supersedes the provider-first recommendation in the initial feasibility note. No application or production changes were made.

## Finding

**Yes: an independent results pipeline and our own chamber-control model are feasible.** Direct official data is substantially more accessible than the initial assessment established. I downloaded and parsed current-election exports from North Carolina, Arizona, Georgia, Virginia and Texas, covering **661 counties or county-equivalent local jurisdictions** between them. North Carolina, Arizona, Georgia and Virginia expose precinct and voting-method detail; Texas exposes county-by-contest totals in the inspected payload. All five currently contain zero votes, as expected before polls close. These are verified schemas and coverage scaffolds, not evidence that live updates have already worked.

**Literal, complete, automated coverage of every competitive race tonight is not established.** Some election authorities publish through county or town sites, some files are not yet available, and some sites block automated access from this research machine. Maine explicitly describes results that can be announced or physically posted locally. An all-official, comprehensive operation needs a manual collection fallback for that tail, or must disclose incomplete coverage.

The recommended product remains our own forecast. Use officials' raw returns, build our own estimates of the remaining vote, and feed those into joint House and Senate simulations. Commercial hosting of an official results website does not turn those raw returns into someone else's forecast.

## How large is the competitive scope?

As a reproducible sizing exercise, I inspected our public [House](https://535.wtf/house) and [Senate](https://535.wtf/senate) tables and selected races whose first displayed favored probability was at most 95%. That produces **111 House races and 14 Senate races across 33 states**. These are rounded display values, not a definitive competitive classification; the audit retains the selected races.

Our own probabilities must not be the sole watchlist filter. Independent race ratings and a buffer of apparently safer races should expand it. For example, the public [Cook House ratings](https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/house-race-ratings) identify competitive races in Kentucky, New Mexico and Nevada, which were absent from that particular model-based selection. Including those expands the geographic planning envelope to at least 36 states. A final election roster and county/district incidence matrix remain to be built.

For accountability, the 33-state sizing set is: AK, AL, AR, AZ, CA, CO, FL, GA, IA, IN, KS, LA, MD, ME, MI, MN, MO, MS, MT, NC, NE, NH, NJ, NY, OH, OK, PA, SC, TN, TX, VA, WA, WI. Arkansas, Maryland, Missouri, Oklahoma and Tennessee were not source-audited in this pass. Colorado, Alabama, Indiana and Mississippi were investigated only to official index/documentation level; a current general-election live connector was not established. This report is a feasibility investigation and partial source audit, not a claim of complete coverage.

The right unit of work is **a reusable parser plus election-specific configurations and coverage checks**. AZ, GA, VA and Kent demonstrate substantial reuse. Other families include tab-delimited files, NIST-style XML, Clarity, custom JSON and PDF reports. That avoids a bespoke scraper for every county, but hundreds of local configurations are still plausible once Michigan and municipality-based states are included. The exact count depends on validated statewide aggregation and the chosen House roster.

For a competitive Senate race, all counties matter, including heavily partisan ones. For a House race, all reporting units inside the district matter. Where returns already identify the congressional contest, assignment to that race is straightforward; matching historical precinct performance to the 2026 district boundaries is a separate task. State and county copies of the same votes must never be added together.

## What makes this our own needle

The value is our estimate of **how much vote remains, where it is, and how it is likely to split**. Our current repository already supplies polling priors and correlated chamber simulations. It does not yet supply a likelihood for partial returns, precinct-level baselines, or an election-night data pipeline.

For each geography and voting method, model remaining turnout and candidate shares with uncertainty. Compare incoming results with the expected performance of that particular geography/method, then update race, state and national effects jointly. Draw remaining votes across all unresolved races and calculate the fraction of simulations reaching a chamber majority. Simply replacing individual race probabilities while drawing the common national error afresh from its pre-election distribution would miss an essential part of learning from the count.

The model must represent uncovered areas explicitly. A partial collection of urban counties is not a random sample of a Senate electorate. Likewise, precincts reporting is not the fraction of ballots counted, and an early-vote batch should not be extrapolated as though it were Election Day voting. Retain prior uncertainty where information is absent, disclose which races have usable returns, and avoid presenting a locally updated model as fully observed nationally.

Maine and Alaska need ranked-choice handling. Senate control must use caucus affiliation rather than party labels alone; Nebraska's inspected export labels Dan Osborn with a petition-party code. Keep all 435 House seats and the full Senate election/holdover roster in the joint model, even when detailed collection focuses on a competitive subset. Safe seats should not silently become certain.

Our existing simulator's configuration records a **133-second production median** for the full polling-forecast run on the shared 2 GB host. That makes a separate, efficient live computation path preferable to rerunning the full polling/newsroom pipeline every few seconds. This is a repository observation, not a new production benchmark. Relevant code: `app/lib/forecast/race_model.rb`, `app/lib/forecast/simulator.rb`, `app/lib/forecast/runner.rb`, `app/jobs/forecast/run_job.rb`, and `config/model_params.yml`.

## Collection and operating design

1. **Source registry:** official provenance, election ID/date, contest and candidate IDs, reporting-unit hierarchy, ballot-method definitions, expected coverage, polling policy and fallback source. Reconcile one complete pre-election roster before accepting totals.
2. **Independent collectors:** prefer advertised downloads; poll on a source-specific schedule, commonly minutes. Honor Arizona's two-minute minimum interval. Use compression, conditional requests, backoff and one shared fetch per source, independent of page traffic. Large statewide exports make careless polling wasteful.
3. **Immutable raw snapshots:** retain source publication time, retrieval time, hash and original payload in object storage. Preserve revisions, including legitimate downward corrections. Use one authoritative source per geographic component at a time, with explicit replacement rules when a county is fresher than the state.
4. **Validation and normalization:** reject test elections and wrong dates; distinguish unreported from zero; reconcile child/parent totals without double counting. Keep reporting status separate from votes. Quarantine unexplained schema or coverage changes, while unaffected sources continue updating.
5. **Separate inference and publication:** produce one atomic model snapshot with input versions, coverage and uncertainty. Serve cached JSON to the needle page, which can refresh more frequently than the upstream files. Show the source timestamp and model timestamp separately.
6. **Human operation:** a small admin view for missing jurisdictions, stale feeds, conflicting reports and reviewed manual entries. For a physical/phone report, preserve provenance and use a second check before publication. A manual collection workflow would require staffing and arrangements; none were made during this research.

Public data access removes a mandatory national vendor dependency for the verified sources. It does not eliminate engineering, bandwidth, historical-data preparation or election-night staffing costs. No blanket public-data license or unlimited request permission is assumed.

## Effort and recommendation

These are planning estimates, not measured delivery commitments. They assume developers familiar with this app, source access comparable to the successful probes, and a deliberately limited initial model.

| Scope | Estimated work | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Archive/normalize the five verified state exports and Kent; display totals and coverage | Roughly 1–3 engineer-days; a smaller archival collector is plausible today | A useful independent data foundation. It does not alone produce calibrated probabilities. |
| A limited experimental nowcast using those areas and explicit priors elsewhere | Several additional days for an experienced modeling/engineering pair, with substantial uncertainty | Our own updating model, with clearly bounded coverage and limited validation. A hurried tonight-only version would be an experiment. |
| Direct coverage of the broad competitive roster, local fallbacks and operational hardening | Several engineer-weeks; a preliminary staffing assumption is 2 engineers for 2–4 weeks, plus manual-collection help | A planning range that can widen if key localities lack timely online publishing. Cannot be promised as a complete same-day build. |
| A defensible national chamber needle | Additional modeling, geography preparation and replay work; calendar time depends on historical snapshots and failure cases | Probability calibration and robustness, not just functioning ingestion. No precise completion date is justified yet. |

**Recommendation:** build our own, beginning with NC/AZ/GA/VA/TX and Kent as the initial verified-source set. Treat Ohio as pending its switch out of test mode. Prioritize Iowa, Kansas, Michigan's remaining counties, Maine and New Hampshire next because unresolved Senate coverage can dominate the chamber estimate. Expand House coverage by district importance and overlap with already supported feeds, using an external watchlist as a guardrail.

For today, the strongest achievable foundation is a collector that archives real reporting sequences and a transparent results/coverage view. An experimental partial-coverage needle could follow, with the uncertainty and missing areas visible. Complete automatic official-only coverage of every competitive race tonight is not demonstrated by this research. That limitation does not undermine the longer-term feasibility of owning the model and the collection pipeline.

Validation should use timestamped historical night snapshots where available, not shuffled final precinct results. Test delayed mail batches, missing rural or urban coverage, revised totals, test-to-production transitions, redistricting, ranked-choice transfers and independent caucus outcomes. Evaluate calibration and interval coverage as well as ingestion correctness. The final NC 2024 file is a baseline resource, not a substitute for that replay.

## Verification boundaries

Research used public official pages, current downloadable payloads and the local repository. Downloads were one-off samples before polls closed. No provider was contacted, no subscriptions were purchased, no credentials were inspected, no messages were sent to officials, no polling automation was started, and no site was deployed. Source accessibility can differ by network and can change during election night. The accompanying audit distinguishes parsed current files, test data, documentation-only leads and access failures.
